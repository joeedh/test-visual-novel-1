A public plaza built for the display of state power: a broad flagged square hemmed by government facades, a raised iron scaffold at its heart, broadcast-horns and scrying-plates mounted high so the whole quarter can be made to watch. Assayer grey and governmental brass dominate the architecture. Crowd-barriers, banners of a hostile regime, and the cold theatrical lighting create a stage where punishment is never private. In daylight it is grimly ordinary; lit for an execution, it becomes the place where the brutal reality of the stranded pair's fate is made undeniable, serving as the branch point for the story's routes.
