Open sand-and-slate training grounds behind the furnace hall, ringed by blast-baffles of riveted iron and rack after rack of Governor gauntlets. Chalked warding circles scar the ground; the air wavers with heat and the geometric cold-shimmer of competing currents. Here the cautious drill their metered weaves within the lines while the reckless — the ones who bare rust rather than hide it — burn scorch-marks past the baffles. It is the one place at the college where raw power is on open, dangerous display.
