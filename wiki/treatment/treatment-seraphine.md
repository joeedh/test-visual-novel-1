# Treatment — Seraphine Halcyon route

The diplomat's path. Where the outline sketches the beats, this treatment expands them into a scene-level throughline. The spine is Seraphine's struggle between the peace-broker role her mother groomed her for and her hunger to choose her own thread — and the slow discovery that the courts she was raised to trust are complicit in the war for who controls the Weave.

## Act one — The watched daughter

Caedon arrives at the Cog-College among a cohort of licensed talents and finds Seraphine Halcyon at the center of every room without seeming to seek it: silver-blonde, unhurried, cloud-grey eyes that miss nothing. She is surrounded by Loomwright-inclined peers and a rotating attendance of envoys who treat a student as a piece on a board. Caedon is charmed past her practiced court reserve precisely because he wants nothing from her house — a novelty she does not know what to do with.

In class he watches her braid Spirit and Air with a delicacy that unnerves the instructors, who are used to force, not thread. He glimpses the peace-broker future her mother has arranged and the quiet resentment beneath her grace. Their early scenes trade in wit and indirection; she tests whether he is another courtier and finds, to her unease, that he is not.

The friend group is sent to investigate the ruins as a test. When nefarious actors intervene, it is Seraphine's perceptiveness that reads the ambush and her diplomacy that turns a losing fight — talking, misdirecting, and weaving where the others would have swung. They emerge victorious, but she alone recognizes the intervention as no accident: someone with reach arranged it. Caedon is drawn into the tangle around her — rival houses courting Halcyon Reach, a treaty whose fine print hides who truly means to leash the Weave.

## Act two — Far from the Reach

Caedon trains in the woods with Seraphine at his side; they learn to read each other's threads until each can feel the other's intent before it forms. It is the closest she has come to being known rather than watched.

Then something goes wrong in the world. The school intensifies training and teleports the students in pairs to random places. Caedon lands with Seraphine — inside a foreign court where her name is both shield and target. On the run from security services, they survive on her fluency in court protocol and cipher; she can open a sealed door with a phrase and read a room's true allegiances at a glance. Working the diplomatic underlayer, they uncover the truth behind the world's collapse: a coordinated design masked as coincidence, treaties written to concentrate control of the Weave.

Word reaches them that another stranded pair, fleeing in a neighboring country, was caught, interrogated, and publicly executed. The grief hardens Seraphine: she stops believing she can broker her way out, and resolves to act rather than mediate.

## Act three — The coalition

Seraphine's blood and bearing open doors closed to everyone else. Where Caedon exposes corruption at the highest levels, she weaves the fallout into alliances — turning scandal into leverage and leverage into a coalition. Some stranded pairs murder the monarchs of the nations they landed in; others build lines of communication back to Caedon, and Seraphine braids them into a single front.

Together they infiltrate the organization behind the chaos, fighting several battles as the allies she brokered join them and the surviving pairs converge for the final fight against the boss. The peace-broker's daughter wins not by force alone but by having made the enemy's own courts turn against it.

Caedon marries Seraphine — who at last chose her own thread rather than the one spun for her. The end.
