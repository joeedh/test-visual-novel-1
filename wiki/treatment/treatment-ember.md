# Treatment — Ember Kellan Route

Expanded story treatment for the route where Caedon's primary love interest is **Ember Kellan**, the Kiln-Sworn forge-prodigy of the Vashan Dominion. The throughline is raw, ungoverned Vash power set against Ferran licensing — and the slow shadow of the Rust that will one day take her. It is a romance lived under a sentence: Ember loves fiercely because she does not expect to love long, and the tragedy of the route is Caedon's refusal to accept that verdict.

## Act One — The Spark

Caedon arrives at the Cog-College among a licensed, metered intake and is immediately drawn to the one student who refuses to be measured. Ember Kellan channels raw current in the practice yards where the cautious hang back — copper-haired, broad-shouldered, her right forearm already tracing the first bloom of rust-scarring, which she bares rather than hides. Her reckless brilliance both alarms and magnetizes him.

He learns her position: she is the Vashan Dominion's test case, a Kiln-Sworn heresy the Ferran licensers mean either to tame or to make an example of. She resents both her keepers and the collar. Among the other male students Caedon finds his footing; with Ember he finds a live wire.

In class, Caedon watches Ember chafe against metered channeling — she can do the disciplined thing and refuses to, believing power is meant to be spent, not hoarded. He begins to see the rust-scarring not as damage but as a dare she throws at the world.

The friend group is sent to investigate ruins as part of a test. When nefarious external actors intervene, Ember spends power without counting the cost, saving the group but burning herself doing it. They emerge victorious. In the aftermath Caedon sees, for the first time, the true price of her recklessness — and that she paid it gladly for them.

Caedon is drawn into the conflict around Ember: the Dominion's proprietary interest in their prodigy, the Ferran suspicion that ungoverned Vash strength can never be safely licensed, and the open question of whether she can be controlled at all — or whether anyone has the right to try.

## Act Two — Spend, Don't Meter

In the woods, training turns intimate. Ember teaches Caedon her heresy: to spend rather than meter, to trust the current instead of leashing it. It thrills and frightens him in equal measure, and it is the first thing that is truly theirs. Between the lessons, she lets slip — never as complaint — how little time she believes the Rust will leave her.

Something goes wrong in the world. The College intensifies training and then teleports the students in pairs across the globe. Caedon lands with Ember, far from any forge that would shelter her kind.

On the run, Ember's raw power is both shield and beacon: it saves them from pursuit and draws more of it. They piece together the truth behind the world's collapse. Meanwhile another stranded pair is caught, interrogated, and publicly executed. Ember, who claims not to fear her own death, takes this one harder than she lets on — it is proof of the thing she has always believed, that time runs out, and it lands on Caedon like a warning about her.

## Act Three — The Rust

Caedon and Ember, with the remaining stranded pairs, seek alliances. Ember's Kiln-Sworn ties and forge-craft open doors in the Vashan Dominion that no licensed talent could; while Caedon exposes corruption at the highest levels, she arms and equips their growing coalition. Some stranded pairs murder the monarchs of the nations they landed in; others build lines of communication back to Caedon.

They infiltrate the organization behind the chaos, fighting several battles. In each Ember spends her strength recklessly, and the Rust creeps further up her arm — the cost of every victory written on her body. Caedon's arc becomes a fight against her fatalism as much as against the enemy. Their recruited allies and the surviving pairs converge for the final assault on the boss.

The boss falls. Ember lives — and marrying her is Caedon's argument, made in deed, against the sentence she has carried her whole life: that she will love and lose quickly. She loves fiercely because she never expected to love long; he stays to prove long is possible. The end.
