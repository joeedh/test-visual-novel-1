# Treatment — Wren Ashfield Route

A story of trust extended to someone who has never been able to afford it. Wren Ashfield is a burned-out channeler and cloudlane smuggler of the Ashen Circle underground; the emotional engine of the route is whether she can believe Caedon will not sell her, and whether he can prove worthy of a loyalty that has only ever cost her.

## Act One — The Outsider

Caedon arrives at the Cog-College a half-step out of place: a talent without the polish of the licensed houses. Among the intake he keeps colliding with Wren, a scholarship outsider who moves through the College's rules as if they were weather to be slipped, not obeyed. She is quick-eyed, guarded, and openly contemptuous of anyone official-looking — she assumes they will hand her to the Grey Hands the moment it profits them.

In class Caedon sees the machinery of punishment up close: ungoverned channeling is a crime, and the College trains its students partly to police it. Wren sits with her Rust-scarred hands gloved and hidden, answering nothing she isn't forced to. Caedon notices, and — crucially — says nothing, keeps her secret without being asked. This is the first coin of trust.

The friend group is sent to investigate a set of ruins as a field test. The 'nefarious external actors' who intervene turn out not to be rival students but Grey Hand Assayers hunting a fugitive channeler who fled into the same ruins. The test becomes a real escape. Wren's smuggling contacts — a cloudlane cache, a bolt-hole — are the only reason the group gets out alive. They emerge victorious, but Wren's cover is torn: she has used the Circle's resources in front of witnesses, and the Assayers now have her scent.

Caedon is drawn fully into the conflict that surrounds her: the Assayers' hunt for severed and unlicensed channelers, and the Ashen Circle that smuggles the discarded to safety.

## Act Two — On the Cloudlanes

Woodland training becomes something else when Wren takes over: she teaches Caedon the smugglers' cloudlane craft the College refuses to — reading air currents, moving unseen, spending power without a Governor's leash. Their intimacy grows in shared risk, not in words.

Then the world tips. Something goes catastrophically wrong on the geopolitical scale; the school intensifies training and, when things worsen, teleports the students out in pairs to scatter them beyond reach. Caedon lands with Wren — in hostile territory where her kind are shot on sight, and where her scars mark her instantly.

On the run, the roles invert: it is Wren's underground, not Caedon's schooling, that keeps them alive. Bolt-holes, forged tokens, smuggler debts called in. Threading through the fugitive networks, they piece together the truth behind the chaos: it is bound up in the Assayers' program of mass severing — the severed are not merely policed but harvested. Meanwhile, word reaches them that another stranded pair was caught, interrogated, and publicly executed. The atrocity lands hard: it is exactly the fate Wren has run from all her life, made real for someone they knew.

## Act Three — The Discarded Rise

Caedon and Wren, with the surviving stranded pairs, begin building a coalition. Wren's value is singular: she opens doors among the discarded and the smuggler-nets that no monarch commands. Caedon exposes corruption at the highest levels, feeding the evidence into the networks Wren threads. Some stranded pairs assassinate the rulers of the nations they landed in; others establish lines of communication back to Caedon.

With the Ashen Circle at their backs, Caedon and Wren infiltrate the organization behind the chaos, fighting through several set-piece battles. The recruited allies and the surviving pairs converge for the final assault, and the discarded — the people the world threw away — are the ones who bring it down.

The route ends not in triumph alone but in something Wren never let herself want: Caedon marries her, and for the first time she is trusted, wholly, by someone who never sold her. The end.
