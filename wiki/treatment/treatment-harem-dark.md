# Treatment — The Undivided (Dark Polygamy Arc)

A subversion of the harem-game trope. Caedon never lets himself fall for one love interest, so he falls for all four — and that undivided devotion curdles into conquest. This route diverges from the four romance paths at the moment a stranded pair dies, and it ends not in a wedding but in a ruined world. The through-line is love as instrument: what begins as refusal to choose becomes refusal to lose, and refusal to lose becomes tyranny.

## Act One — The Cataloguer

Caedon arrives at the Cog-College among the intake and is drawn to Wren, Isolde, Seraphine, and Ember alike — but commits to none. Where another protagonist would gravitate toward one heart, he treats each closeness as strategy rather than surrender, holding all four at the same careful distance while letting each believe she is seen. He falls in with the wider friend group and the other male students, and is liked by everyone precisely because he belongs wholly to no one.

In class he is quietly cataloguing. He watches Wren slip the College's leash with ungoverned smuggler's craft, Isolde leash her cold Sela precision behind piety, Seraphine braid Spirit and Air with a Loomwright's unnerving delicacy, and Ember spend raw Vash current in the yards without counting the cost. He files each strength away — not as a suitor weighing a match, but as a strategist inventorying assets.

The friend group is sent to investigate the ruins as part of a test. When the nefarious external actors intervene, Caedon does what no single-route Caedon can: he coordinates all four love interests at once, each covering the others' weaknesses, and they emerge victorious with unnerving efficiency. He begins to see the four of them not as choices to be made but as instruments that work best in concert — and to see himself as the hand that conducts them.

He is drawn into the conflicts surrounding all four at once — the Grey Hands' hunt, Vorn's watchers, Halcyon's courts, the Dominion's heresy — and refuses to pick a side or a heart in any of them, keeping every door open.

## Act Two — The Break

Caedon trains alone in the woods, sharpening himself relentlessly. Where the romance routes soften here into confidences and closeness, he hardens: he is building a self, not a bond.

Something goes wrong in the world. The school intensifies training, then teleports the students in pairs to random places. **This is where the route branches from the four romance paths.** One of the other stranded pairs is caught, interrogated, and publicly executed — and this time Caedon witnesses or learns the full brutality of it, in detail the other routes spare him. The death breaks something in him. Having never given his heart to one person, he now vows he will lose *none* of them, and that the world which killed them will answer for it. The refusal to choose becomes a refusal to lose.

On the run, Caedon no longer hunts the truth in order to survive — he hunts it in order to punish. He reunites with the love interests one by one, binding each to him not through romance alone but through shared vengeance and the promise that he can keep them all alive where the world could not. Wren opens the smuggler-nets, Isolde turns her Assayer knowledge inward, Seraphine reads the courts, Ember arms them. All four come to love him — and, for the first time, he lets himself love all four. It is genuine, and that is what makes it dangerous: his love is total, and so his vengeance is total.

## Act Three — The Warlord

Caedon and the four seek alliances, but he uses them ruthlessly. He exposes corruption not to reform the nations but to destabilize and seize them. Where the romance routes' stranded pairs murder monarchs out of desperation, Caedon kills them as conquest, absorbing their forces into a growing host. The coalition-building of the other routes becomes empire-building here.

He develops and deploys strategic-class weaves — weapons on the order of nukes — to utterly destroy the organization behind the world's chaos. Isolde's precision aims them, Ember's raw current powers them, Seraphine's braided subtlety hides them, and Wren's forbidden craft smuggles them into place: the four instruments he catalogued in Act One, all turned to a single monstrous working. Along the way he bloodily conquers one of the empires, crowning himself and his four consorts over its ashes.

The final boss falls — not to a hero, but to a warlord. There is no wedding. There is a dynasty.

## Epilogue — The Timeskip

Years later. Caedon's many children, born to all four consorts, fight a bloody civil war over his inheritance. The strategic weaves he pioneered are turned on cities. The world he conquered to protect the people he loved is left in ruins by the very children of that love — the harem fantasy followed to its logical, catastrophic end. The end.
