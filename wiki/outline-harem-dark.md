# Route: The Undivided (Dark Polygamy Arc)

A subversion of the harem-game trope. If Caedon never lets himself fall for any single love interest, he falls for all of them — and the shared devotion curdles into something monstrous. This route diverges from the four romance paths at the moment a stranded pair dies, and it does not end in a wedding but in a ruined world.

## first act

Caedon arrives at the academy and meets his fellow students and teachers. He is drawn to Wren, Isolde, Seraphine, and Ember alike, but commits to none — treating each closeness as strategy rather than surrender. He gravitates toward the whole friend group and the other male students.

Caedon goes to class, quietly cataloguing everyone's strengths: Wren's ungoverned smuggler's craft, Isolde's cold Sela precision, Seraphine's Loomwright subtlety, Ember's reckless Vash current.

The friend group teams up to investigate the ruins as part of a test. Nefarious external actors intervene; Caedon coordinates all four love interests together and they emerge victorious. He begins to see the four of them not as choices but as instruments that work best in concert.

Caedon is drawn into the conflicts surrounding all four at once, refusing to choose a side or a heart.

## second act

Caedon goes training in the woods, sharpening himself relentlessly.

Something goes wrong in the world. The school intensifies training, and the students are teleported in pairs to random places. **This is where the route branches from the others:** one of the other stranded pairs is caught, interrogated, and publicly executed — and this time Caedon witnesses or learns the full brutality of it. The death breaks something in him. Having never given his heart to one person, he now vows that he will lose *none* of them, and that the world that killed them will answer for it.

On the run, Caedon no longer discovers the truth in order to survive — he hunts it in order to punish. He reunites with the love interests one by one, binding them to him not through romance alone but through shared vengeance. All four come to love him, and he lets himself love all four.

## third act

Caedon and the love interests seek out alliances, but he uses them ruthlessly — exposing corruption not to reform the nations but to destabilize and seize them. Where other pairs murdered monarchs out of desperation, Caedon does it as conquest, absorbing their forces.

He develops and deploys strategic-class weaves — weapons on the order of nukes — to utterly destroy the organization behind the chaos in the world. Isolde's precision, Ember's raw current, Seraphine's braided subtlety, and Wren's forbidden craft are all turned to the working. Along the way he bloodily conquers one of the empires, crowning himself and his four consorts over its ashes.

The final boss falls not to a hero but to a warlord. There is no wedding — there is a dynasty.

## epilogue: the timeskip

Years later. Caedon's many children, born to all four consorts, fight a bloody civil war over his inheritance. The strategic weaves he pioneered are turned on cities. The world he conquered to protect the people he loved is left in ruins by the very children of that love. The end.
